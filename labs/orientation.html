About the course

Course Description

From the syllabus

The Programming Logic and Design (PLD) laboratory course introduces students to the foundational principles of computer programming logic, algorithmic thinking, and structured program design. Without requiring prior coding experience, students learn to formulate algorithms using flowcharts and pseudocode before implementing functional, well-documented programs in C++.

Core topics

  • Data types
  • Operators
  • Sequential, selection & iterative control
  • Arrays
  • References & pointers
  • Functions
  • Intro to OOP
  • Debugging

Final course output

Design, develop, document, and demonstrate a modular C++ program that solves a specified engineering or real-world problem using appropriate data structures, control structures, and algorithms.

Suggested time: about 4 min

Teaching tips

  • Emphasize "no prior coding experience required": Labs 1–4 build the foundation slowly.
  • Mention the final output early so students see where the 15 labs lead.

What you will be able to do

Course Outcomes & How They Are Measured

CO1 · 35%

Formulate & construct

Formulate step-by-step algorithms using flowcharts and pseudocode, and use modern C++ development environments following proper coding standards to construct structured programs.

CO2 · 50%

Analyze, test & debug

Analyze, test, and debug C++ programs through systematic code tracing and test cases to verify logical correctness across sequential, selection, iterative, modular, and basic OOP constructs.

CO3 · 15%

Design & present

Design, implement, document, and present a modular C++ program that solves basic engineering or real-world problems using appropriate algorithms and data structures.

Class & laboratory policies

Policies You Need to Know

1 · Attendance (BulSU Student Handbook)

  • Tardy: arriving 15 minutes after the start. Absent: arriving 20 minutes after.
  • More than 20% unexcused absences before the midterm means you are dropped (FDA/UD).
  • Excused absences (official representation, certified sickness, force majeure) should not exceed 30%, and requirements still apply.
  • Absent without valid reason during an exam or activity: score of zero.

2 · Submission of requirements

  • Late submissions lose 10% per day, up to 3 days. More than 3 days late is not accepted without prior approval.
  • Graded work is returned with feedback within 14 calendar days (Student Assessment Return and Acknowledgment Form).
  • Compile all activities into your student portfolio.

3 · Academic integrity

  • Submit your own work and cite your sources. No plagiarism, cheating, or copying.
  • Use AI tools only when allowed, and disclose how you used them.
  • Attach the signed Academic Integrity Statement to every exam and output.

4–6 · Safe space, accommodations, course rules

  • Respect everyone; ask permission before recording or sharing others' photos, messages, or work.
  • Need support (health, disability, other concerns)? Talk to the professor privately and early.
  • Gadgets for course-related activities only; silence phones.
  • Check MS Teams, the Messenger group, and email regularly. Use professional language.

Suggested time: about 6 min

Teaching tips

  • AI tools: the Academic Integrity box states the syllabus rule (use only when allowed, and disclose). Announce any task-specific AI conditions in class or on the task.
  • Consultation hours: by appointment (per the syllabus).

7 · Computer laboratory

Computer Laboratory Policies

Do

  • Use the lab only as scheduled and with the instructor's consent.
  • Wear your school ID; place bags on the designated shelves.
  • Check your station for missing peripherals or vandalism before class. You are responsible for your assigned computer.
  • Report a non-working computer to your instructor.
  • Work quietly; phones on silent.
  • Inform your instructor if you bring your own laptop (computer user monitoring form).
  • Shut down the computer and return the chair, mouse, and keyboard to their places before leaving.

Don't

  • Use lab computers for games, music, videos, or movies.
  • Install software or change hardware or system configuration.
  • Eat, drink, chew gum, smoke, or vandalize.
  • Swap or remove computer codes or tags.
  • Insert USB drives or other storage without the instructor's permission.

Damage, loss, or tampered equipment is charged to the last user and/or all class users of the room, and is subject to disciplinary action.
